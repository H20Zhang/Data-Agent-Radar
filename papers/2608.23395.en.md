# VAT decomposition: testing agent granularity with falsification criteria

[中文](2608.23395.md) | **English** · [Home](../README.en.md) · [Research Library](../library/README.en.md)

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23395) · [Code, data, and traces](https://github.com/pedro-santos-eng/Right-sizing-LLM-agent-decomposition-in-VAT-determination) · [Archived artifact](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22083282) · **Importance: 4/5**

> **Research delta.** This 4,400-run pilot holds VAT subtasks, tools, schemas, validation, merge policy, and base model fixed while changing how five dependent subtasks are assigned to one through five workers. Token-matched sensitivity and three failure injections test whether apparent multi-agent gains instead reflect budget or recovery behavior.

## Problem

Multi-agent systems often change worker count together with prompt budget, context segmentation, ledgers, localized retry, and validation. A higher score therefore does not identify whether the cause is agent count, a dependency boundary, or extra compute. The paper decomposes closed-world VAT determination into classification, jurisdiction, rate, exemption, and reverse-charge synthesis, then sweeps worker partitions.

## Mechanism

The four orchestrated conditions C1–C4 share a deterministic Magentic-One-style orchestrator, task/progress ledgers, typed records, schema validation, and dependency-order dispatch. C1 assigns all five subtasks to one worker; C2 and C3 place one or two boundaries near the middle of the dependency chain; C4 uses one worker per subtask. S0 is a tuned monolith without the orchestrator: it emits a complete trace and can repair the whole trace up to three times. The deterministic rule engine supplies synthetic oracle labels; it is not a competing system.

The release contains 40 evaluation cases, 8 development cases, the oracle, prompts, injection plan, 4,400 canonical raw traces, and scoring and analysis code. It also preserves 2,400 superseded traces from pre-fix attempts. Falsification criteria precede result commits in the current repository history, but the history was rewritten and three harness defects were repaired after runs followed by complete reruns. The defensible description is **repository-precommitted criteria with disclosed repairs**, not an externally sealed preregistration.

## Closest comparison

C1–C4 are the closest worker-count comparison: they share the orchestrator and activity surface and change the partition of five tasks. Partitions still alter context segmentation, handoff exposure, and the unit of localized recovery. S0 and C1 both use one worker, but also differ in orchestration, ledgers, dispatch, validation localization, and repair state, so their contrast does not isolate orchestration alone.

## Decisive evidence

- Each main condition covers 40 cases × 5 repeats. Accuracy point estimates are S0 **0.755**, C1 **0.720**, C2 **0.830**, C3 **0.830**, and C4 **0.770**. C3 uses about 14% more tokens and has about 42% higher median latency than S0.
- **None of four pre-specified headline tests survives Holm correction.** C2–C1 has a paired interval of +0.110 [+0.010,+0.210] but Holm-adjusted p=.172; intervals for C2/C3 versus C4 cross zero. The authors therefore mark the intermediate-optimum hypothesis **unsupported** under their own criterion.
- Token-matched S0′ is 6.5 points below C2, but the interval [−0.165,+0.025] crosses zero. It neither shows that prompt budget explains the gap nor establishes an independent partition effect.
- A one-shot tool outage is usually absorbed by every configuration. C1's +16-point timeout result comes with roughly 2.9× total tokens and is better read as fault-triggered resampling than free recovery.
- Injecting one schema-conforming, oracle-wrong record sharply degrades every condition: S0 to **0.560**, C1 to **0.405**, C2/C3 to **0.345**, and C4 to **0.310**. Schema and citation-presence validation is nearly powerless against business-content errors.

## Cost and failure boundary

Mean token-derived cost per main-sweep case is approximately $0.0181 for S0, $0.0172 for C1, $0.0220 for C2, $0.0210 for C3, and $0.0238 for C4. Prices are fixed to the paper's service snapshot and are not durable deployment costs. Latency is median case latency under capped concurrency, with no p95/p99; forced timeouts raise immediately and do not model provider waiting or billing.

The content-fault comparison also has a timing confound. S0/C1 are overwritten after a complete trace already exists, while handoff-bearing conditions may let downstream workers consume the corrupted record. The study supports the danger of schema-valid semantic corruption in handoffs, but does not cleanly establish that more agents are intrinsically more fragile.

## Claim ceiling

The work shows how a richly released synthetic pilot can jointly test decomposition point estimates, budget alternatives, and fault propagation. Its strongest result is negative: current validation fails to stop schema-conforming business-content errors. It does not establish an intermediate optimum, multi-agent superiority, successful removal of budget confounding, or equivalence between the synthetic oracle and real VAT or legal truth.

## Field-map consequence

This work enters **Planning & Semantic Interaction** as an `early_signal` and connects to Verification & Recovery. It makes dependency-layer partition placement a testable control surface, but establishes no trend and leaves the stable Field Map unchanged.

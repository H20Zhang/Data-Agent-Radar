# TRACE: Separate Evidence Discovery from Catalog Publication

[中文](2608.20844.md) | **English** · [Home](../README.en.md) · [Research Library](../library/README.en.md)

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20844) · **Importance: 4/5**

> **Research delta.** TRACE uses a ScoutAgent to combine seller catalogs, syndicated feeds, images, and identity-matched web evidence, then a JudgeAgent to reassess product identity and attribute support before a write policy publishes, blocks, or sends the value to review.

## Problem

Missing catalog attributes are often buried in unstructured content or external pages. Similar models and variants can produce plausible values belonging to the wrong product, so successful extraction is not automatically a publishable catalog fact.

## Mechanism

A category template chooses priority attributes. ScoutAgent collects and normalizes multi-source evidence, returning candidate values, citations, confidence, and abstention/conflict states. JudgeAgent independently rechecks identity and support, may search again, and emits PASS, FAIL, UNVERIFIED, or UNCERTAIN. The write policy blocks low confidence and FAIL, sends UNCERTAIN to humans, but permits PASS or UNVERIFIED writes.

## Closest comparison

There is no Scout-only, no-Judge, single-agent, or deterministic-gate control. Holding the Judge fixed while swapping Scout backbones compares model packages. The online A/B test isolates display of the complete TRACE output, not JudgeAgent, multi-agent decomposition, search, or provenance.

## Decisive evidence

- The Grocery/Alcohol human set contains 500 SKUs and 2,497 SKU–attribute pairs. Scout extraction reaches **98.2%** accuracy at **74.7%** coverage. This is Scout accuracy, not evidence for the Judge or multi-agent split.
- Among PASS values from the earlier binary Judge, 98.4% are human-confirmed. Yet 87.8% of Judge/human disagreements are false rejections, motivating a later four-verdict design. The current verdicts and UNVERIFIED-write policy are not separately human-validated.
- Of 4,990 Electronics/Home Improvement pairs, 87.8% receive an extracted value and 97.4% of those are PASS or UNVERIFIED. Combining these verdicts as “Judge-supported” is not a 97.4% accuracy result.
- Production deployment covers 31 million SKUs. In a five-week randomized 90/10 A/B test, checkout conversion rises **0.48% relative**, 95% CI [+0.04, +0.92], while the missing/incorrect-item rate falls 1.08% relative. This supports end-to-end product impact, not an internal mechanism.

## Cost and failure boundary

Every eligible SKU needs at least one Scout and one Judge call, and either can issue iterative web searches. The paper reports relative Scout-model cost only—no absolute tokens, latency, web queries, human-review load, or dollars. Refresh and drift costs matter at 31-million-SKU scale.

The main Scout and Judge come from the same model family, enabling correlated errors. Identity-match accuracy, citation correctness, provenance completeness, calibration, and review workload are absent. The online test omits traffic size, randomization unit, absolute baselines, and vertical breakdowns.

JSON contracts and write routing structure execution, but citations are not independently audited for complete support. Allowing UNVERIFIED writes makes the verification threshold a business policy, not a truth guarantee.

## Claim ceiling

The evidence supports a large-scale, multi-source catalog-transformation package with proposal and review stages, and its deployment improves coverage and shopping outcomes. It does not show that a second agent, multi-agent architecture, or Judge policy beats a simpler matched control, nor does it validate UNVERIFIED writes as true across categories.

## Field-map consequence

This is an `early_signal` in **Verification & Recovery**, connected to Data Discovery & Grounding and Execution & Transformation. It separates evidence discovery, candidate transformation, and publication authority; one package-level result does not change the stable map.

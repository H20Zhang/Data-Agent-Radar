# CIPHER: Decoupling Exploration and Selection for Data-Science Test-Time Scaling

[中文](2607.14386.md) | **English** · [Home](../README.en.md) · [Research Library](../library/README.en.md)

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14386) · **Importance: 4/5**

> **Research delta.** CIPHER avoids committing the full data-science trajectory to one early hypothesis. It generates diverse initial states, separates exploration from selection, and allocates execution to multiple candidate paths.

## Problem

Early data-science decisions are high impact but low evidence: feature choices, preprocessing, model family, and analysis direction may only reveal their quality after substantial execution. A single-trajectory agent can spend most of its budget downstream of a bad first decision.

## Mechanism

`task → generate diverse initial states → score/select candidates → execute several trajectories → compare outcomes → return best artifact`

The extra test-time compute is spent on **hypothesis coverage**, not only on longer reasoning inside one trajectory.

## Closest comparison

The clean comparison is not simply “CIPHER vs one agent.” It is:

`one trajectory with more budget` vs `multiple diverse starts + selection`

under the same base model, tools, and total compute. Otherwise parallel branching and the algorithmic idea are confounded.

## Decisive evidence

The paper’s useful systems claim is that test-time scaling for data science can act on **plan/state diversity**. The success of later execution depends heavily on early hypothesis coverage, so allocating compute before committing to one path can matter more than sampling extra final answers.

## What remains unproven

Candidate generation, selection, and parallel execution all cost compute and tools. Cost-matched results are therefore the decisive next step. Selection quality is another boundary: if early proxies correlate poorly with final artifact quality, the system can simply choose the wrong branch more efficiently.

## Field-map consequence

Data-agent planning should expose:

`plan generation × diversity × selection × execution allocation`

rather than treating “the plan” as one fixed object.
